Guides

Practise a language aloud

Dictate in the language you are learning, and SpeakWith writes a translation under your own words. Your sentence stays where you said it, so you can read the pair together and see what you got right. This guide covers the two language settings, input and output, what the recorder shows you while it works, and the three things it cannot do yet.

Which builds have it

Multilingual dictation is pre-release. It is in the direct download of the Mac app (the Gumroad build, with its own engine) and in the iPhone app, which has not reached the App Store yet. The Mac App Store build does not have it. If you installed SpeakWith from the Mac App Store, none of the settings on this page are there.

Choose an input and an output language

There is nothing to turn on first: picking the languages is the whole setup. The walkthrough that follows is written for the Mac; the iPhone has its own section further down.

There are two language settings, and they are deliberately not the same list. The input is Spoken input language, in Settings under Engine: the language the engine listens in. It is set per project, so one project can sit in German while another stays in English. The output is Translate polish into, in Settings under Session AI in the section Feedback and polish: the language a polish writes. Leave it empty and a polish does not translate.

Think of them as a source and a target. Both directions work: dictate in German and read it in English, or dictate in English and read it in German. To practise a language, set the spoken input to the language you are learning and the translation to the one you know, then say your sentence.

If the menus are longer than you need, Settings under Languages has two tick lists, Spoken input and Translate into. They only decide which rows the two menus draw, not what you dictate in or translate into. Your current pick always stays in its menu, even when you untick it.

What the panel and the chip tell you

On the Mac, while you record, the live panel header shows Language: and the language the engine will actually use. It reports what the engine will do, not what the setting says. It can name a specific language, read Automatic when the engine detects the language itself, or read English on an engine that transcribes English alone, where there is nothing to choose.

In the Mac's AI sidebar, the Polish heading carries a small chip that says whether this polish will translate. When translation is off it reads Translate: Off. When it is on, it shows the pair as a source and a target. The chip is there even when translate is off, because knowing that it will not translate is an answer you need too. Press it and the same settings open in one place. The chip is a Mac feature; the iPhone does not have it.

What lands in the day document

Settings under Session AI has a row called In the day document, with two choices. Both versions is the default: your dictation stays where it landed, and the translation is written as a new block under it. This is the shape that makes the pair readable when you are learning a language, and it is the one this guide recommends. Say your sentence in the language you are practising, and the page keeps it with the translation beneath. In the figure above, that translation reads: I want to try something this week.

Translation only clears your own words and leaves the translation standing alone. This is lossy. Your original is gone from the page, and a regenerate afterwards will refuse, because the app finds your original by its words and the words are no longer there. Choose it only when you want the translation and nothing else.

Translating the same take a second time replaces the first translation. It does not stack a second one under it.

On the iPhone

The iPhone app has the same pair of languages, in its own places. Open Settings and choose Live Dictation: the row Spoken input is the language the recorder listens in. Translate polish into is in Settings under Session AI, and the tick lists that shorten the menus are in Settings under Languages. The day document choice, Both versions or Translation only, works the same as on the Mac.

Some things differ. The phone transcribes with Apple Speech on the device, and its recorder has its own language menu rather than a Language: line in a panel header. There is no translate chip on the Polish heading, so the settings rows are the place to check whether a polish will translate. The translation goes to your own OpenAI key; there is no Ollama option on the phone.

What it cannot do yet

On the Mac, not every engine can hear every language. An engine that transcribes English only stays in English whatever the setting says, and the panel header tells you so by reading English.

On the Mac and the iPhone alike, the first take in a new language pauses while the on-device model for that language downloads over the network. It is slow the first time and instant afterwards. The pause is the download, not the app hanging.

On the Mac, dictating in a language other than English turns the live second pass off, because the second pass decodes English only.

Where the translation happens

Transcription happens on your device. The translation does not. It is a language-model call, like the other optional AI features, and it goes to your own OpenAI key. On the Mac it can go instead to a local Ollama model you run yourself; the iPhone uses your OpenAI key only.
