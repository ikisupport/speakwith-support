Corrections

Additional layers of control over what gets written

Recognition is never perfect, so SpeakWith adds a few of its own on top of the engine. Each one decides a little more of what actually reaches the page.

Teach tricky terms by voice.

Some terms come back wrong every time you say them, usually names and jargon. Record a few readings of one and SpeakWith keeps them as reference sound. Each example shows what the engine actually heard. That gives SpeakWith more ways to spot the word, and shows you whether one it keeps missing can be corrected at all.

Special terms

Some words are not spelled the way they sound. Worcestershire is one. List the names and jargon you rely on and live dictation watches for them, widening how each one can be matched so it survives more than one pronunciation. Others are harder still: Siobhan is spelled by Irish rules rather than English ones, so its letters do not map onto English sounds at all. For those you can point a spoken cue at the word instead and say Sierra Bravo to write it. Every substitution that fired is listed in a report.

Hallucination gate

Voice detection on its own cannot always tell a breath from a word, and a recognizer handed one can return a word you never said, sometimes a Yeah or an Okay. The gate waits for the written text, then blocks activations that were never meant as speech. New filler goes on the block list as you meet it, so it stops reaching the page.

What SpeakWith does

A document for every day

Everything you record or type on a given day flows into a single organised document, in the order it happened. That layout makes it easy to start recording your thoughts without worrying about files. Each session can carry a generated title, and those titles become the headings the Outline lists, so a long day stays easy to scan and rearrange. Apply a polished take and the clean paragraphs land where the rough ones were, while any screenshots you inserted along the way stay exactly where you put them. Tomorrow, a fresh one begins.

Related sessions, matched on your Mac

While you talk, SpeakWith compares what you are saying with your earlier days and lists the sessions on the same subject in the AI sidebar. Click a row to open the day it came from. Matching runs on your Mac, after a one-time model download. In the direct (Gumroad) build, a short summary can sit above the list, written by the AI service included with a monthly membership, an OpenAI API key you provide, or a local Ollama model. Practice, every time you speak

The longer you use it, the better you speak.

While you record, a small meter shows the pauses inside what you are saying. Over weeks, the effect adds up. You notice when a thought starts to fragment, you stay on a topic a beat longer, and the next time you do it a little more cleanly. Instant feedback on “uh”, “um”, and other filler words trains you to drop them faster. The longer you use it, the more your speaking and your writing improve.

The text on the other end changes too. Single topics arrive in coherent pieces, and paragraphs hold together, so your daily document is something you can hand to a person or a tool without reshaping it first.

Built for how real work happens

The home base your thinking returns to

Real work runs in parallel. You start something that takes time to come back: a long process, a tool working away, a task you've handed off. Instead of waiting, you begin the next thing, then another. Before long, several are in flight at once.

Tools come and go; your daily document stays. It is the one place that holds every thread in a single file that is yours: what each one is for, where you left it, what to check when it lands. You can keep many things moving at once because you are not holding them all in your head.

On the recorder

Measure your effectiveness

Speech is faster than typing, and the research is specific about how much. Ruan et al., ACM IMWUT (2017), measured speech about 3x faster than an iPhone keyboard for short messages: 153 words per minute spoken against 52 typed, counting the time spent fixing mistakes. A desktop keyboard is faster than a phone keyboard, so read that multiple as the phone case.

Correction rate

The share of dictated words you went back and changed. It only counts corrections you actually made, so the true error rate may be higher.

Special terms

The share of words your special terms corrected. Names, terminology, and words you pronounce your own way. The denominator is the words you dictated, the same count the correction rate uses.

Speaking pace

Your words per minute of actual speech. The pauses while you think are in neither half of the figure, so this is how fast you talk and not how much you get down in an hour.

Audio quality

A score for how well the recognizer is hearing you, on your own audio. How it is measured depends on the engine you run, and the guide explains each.

Words, sentences and characters since you last reset the count: everything you said instead of typing it.

Frequently asked questions

Does SpeakWith need an internet connection?

No. Recording, transcription, organising your daily document, and searching its text all happen on your Mac. SpeakWith does its core work offline. Related sessions on the Mac matches offline too, once its matching model is on your Mac: that is a one-time download under Settings, Pipeline, AI Models. Only the optional AI features need a connection, and only when you turn them on.

Can the optional AI features run locally?

On the Mac, yes. Related sessions always matches locally: the model and the index it searches both live on your Mac, and no setting sends that step anywhere. Session titles stay local when you choose Ollama under Settings, Session AI, and so does the short summary above the Related list when you choose On this Mac under Settings, Related. That summary is in the direct (Gumroad) Mac build. A monthly membership includes a cloud AI service for titles, polish, and the Related summary instead, and you can also use an OpenAI API key you provide. On iPhone and iPad, SpeakWith Pro includes that service for titles and polish. They are optional, and they are not local. Core recording and transcription never need the network.

Where are my recordings and documents stored?

As ordinary files on your Mac. Open, copy, back up, or delete them with any tool you like.

How accurate is the transcription?

Automatic transcription is very good but never perfect. Names and specialist terms are the usual places small errors appear. Your daily document is fully editable, so treat the transcript as an excellent first draft you can correct.

See how the recorder reports your correction rate.

Does using SpeakWith change how I speak?

It can, through quiet feedback. While you record, a small meter shows the pauses inside what you are saying, including each “uh” and “um”, something you can glance at without breaking your train of thought. Watching those marks as you speak is what changes the habit: the same fillers keep appearing on the meter, session after session, until you stop saying them. Over weeks, you may hold a topic together longer and let it land in a single, coherent piece. Your daily document gets easier to work with, and so can the way you speak to people away from the screen.

Can I import audio and video I already have?

Yes. Import a voice memo, a recorded meeting, or a screen recording, and SpeakWith writes it up the same way it writes up a live recording.

Can I keep working while a recording is written up?

Yes. That is one of SpeakWith's best features. Type and paste into the day's document while dictation is still processing; everything ends up in the correct chronological order.

Can I dictate into other apps?

Yes, on the direct (Gumroad) Mac build. Turn on the recorder switch, or set a hotkey in Settings. SpeakWith types each finished phrase into whichever app currently has keyboard focus. Your daily document still receives everything as usual. The sandboxed App Store build cannot do this; macOS does not allow cross-app typing from sandboxed apps.

Is there an iOS app?

Yes. SpeakWith for iOS is on the App Store for iPhone and iPad. Live dictation, day documents, search, and projects are free. SpeakWith Pro adds session titles and polish, with the AI allowance included and a 7-day free trial; the App Store shows the price in your currency. It transcribes on-device with Apple Speech, and it works alongside the Mac app rather than replacing it.

On iOS, the permission prompt says speech is "sent to Apple". Is my voice uploaded?

No. That line is Apple's standard wording for the speech-recognition permission, shown to every app that asks for it. SpeakWith transcribes on-device using Apple's built-in dictation: your audio is turned into text on your iPhone or iPad. The only thing that downloads is the speech model itself, once. After that, transcription runs on your device.

What languages does SpeakWith support?

On the direct Mac build and the iPhone app, Apple Speech opens many spoken input languages: choose the one you dictate in, and pick an output language when you want a translation underneath. On Mac, Parakeet and Nemotron are additional live engines if you prefer them to Apple. The Mac App Store build does not include those engines or language menus.

Is this an audio recorder?

It is a recorder for the words you speak and want turned into text. Think of it as a voice-to-text journal. SpeakWith is tuned for speech and dictation, the way a person actually talks, so it does its best work on the human voice. It is not built for capturing music or ambient sound.

Is the Mac download signed and checked by Apple?

Yes. The direct Mac download is signed with our Apple Developer ID, so the apps you install match the build we shipped. Before upload, Apple's notary service scans that submission for known malicious software and for code-signing problems, and we staple the ticket so Gatekeeper can check it. That scan is not an App Store review, and it is not a promise that the software is free of malware.
